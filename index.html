01 · The problem

The schedule says one thing. The forecast says another. The board hears a third.

Most utility programs do not fail for lack of a plan. They fail because the plan stopped describing the work and nobody said so. The baseline moves. The monthly report lands on Monday and answers nothing. By the time the overspend shows in the actuals, the money is gone. Then the commission asks for the records.

In their words

"The plans and schedules are not reflective of actual project circumstances."

Independent assessment of V.C. Summer Units 2 and 3, filed with the South Carolina Public Service Commission.

"Commissioners don't like surprises, even if it's their birthday."

Nick Wagner, former commissioner, Iowa Utilities Commission.

"Did not result in any documentation is an indication of imprudence."

Missouri Public Service Commission staff, construction audit.

03 · How it works

Eight weeks. One line.

  1. Weeks 1 to 2

    Baseline.

    We take the schedule, the estimate and the risk register you already have and build one approved baseline from them. Scope, cost and schedule, agreed and signed.

  2. Weeks 3 to 5

    Cadence.

    A monthly rhythm that answers four questions: what slipped, what is about to slip, what it cost, and what decision you need to make.

  3. Weeks 6 to 8

    Governance.

    Stage gates, dashboards, and the records that let you show a commission exactly how every dollar was overseen.

Then it runs. Your team owns it. Our advisors stay as long as you want them and not a day longer.
